Algorithm Guide & FAQs ▼

Orbital Trail Curve Fitting addresses a common challenge in game physics, trajectory modeling, and real-time graphics: closely spaced sample points along a large circular trajectory produce nearly flat, collinear chords due to tiny sagitta deflection.

By applying specialized parametric transformations (Bézier amplification, projective weighting, clothoid curvature gradients, or logarithmic spirals), developers can produce expressive, sweeping trails at minimal computational cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do circular orbit sample points look flat?

Short arc intervals have very low geometric deflection (\(h \approx R(1 - \cos(\Delta\theta/2))\)). Curve fitting amplifies the orthogonal normal displacement to restore dynamic curvature.

Which algorithm is fastest for game development?

The Amplified Quadratic Bézier runs in \(O(1)\) closed form (~15 FLOPs) without expensive trigonometric solvers or matrix inversions.

What is the advantage of an Euler spiral (Clothoid)?

Curvature changes linearly with arc length (\(\kappa = \kappa_0 + \alpha s\)), preventing abrupt lateral acceleration and creating smooth transitions.
